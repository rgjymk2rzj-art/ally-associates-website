Kenya 2.7 Moderate · Deteriorating
Coverage full, 3 of 3 layers
Signal Inflation rose to 7.3% in October from 6.8% in September, near the top of the 2.5–7.5% target range. Core inflation rose to 4.3% from 4.0%; diesel was up 32.5% on the year and petrol 22.8%. The Central Bank of Kenya held its rate at 8.75% on 7 October. Reserves are USD 14.7bn, or 5.9 months of imports, and the shilling held at KSh 129.96 per dollar on 5 November. Lake Magadi: Tata Chemicals’ mining and exports have been suspended since 28 July. The President ordered the company out on 3 September; a joint technical committee was formed on 8 September; the suspension remains in force, with a High Court date on 18 November.
Already signalled Core inflation was already rising in September (3.4% to 4.0%) while the headline was cushioned by fuel subsidies and a temporary VAT cut on fuel. The October jump was signalled. Magadi began as a compliance suspension in July, five weeks before the public exit order.
Where the reads disagree Funding conditions are improving while inflation and political risk are rising. Supporters read the Magadi order as industrial policy; the company says it is fully compliant and the court has not yet ruled.
Forward-look With inflation near the top of the band, the December MPC is more likely to hold than cut. The 18 November hearing is the next marker on Magadi.
What would change this read Direction moves to Stable if November inflation falls back and the Magadi dispute settles on published terms. Level rises to Elevated if the CBK has to raise rates.

Ethiopia 3.5 Elevated · Stable
Coverage partial, 2 of 3 layers
Signal The National Bank of Ethiopia held its rate at 16% on 3 November, within a 13–19% corridor. Inflation eased to 14.9% in September from 15.3% in July; non-food inflation rose to 15.1%, driven by electricity tariffs and fuel prices. Reserve money grew 87.2% on the year, mainly from the central bank’s gold operations. Commercial bank credit grew 42.6% after the 24% credit cap was lifted in July.
Already signalled Lifting the credit cap in July and the gold-driven rise in reserve money loosened liquidity months before this hold. The 42.6% credit growth was the visible result.
Where the reads disagree The rate signal is tight while liquidity and credit are expanding fast. Local experts quoted in Capital disagree on whether the next move is a further tightening or targeted liquidity tools.
Forward-look The central bank will absorb liquidity and may impose bank-specific reserve requirements. Inflation is projected to stay in double digits for six months. The next review is at the end of January.
What would change this read Direction moves to Improving if inflation falls below 12% before the January review. Level rises if reserve requirements are tightened bank by bank.

Tanzania 2.5 Moderate · Stable
Coverage full, 3 of 3 layers
Signal The Bank of Tanzania held its rate at 6.25% on 7 October, after raising it from 5.75% in July. It wants the July rise to pass through fully and says El Niño or higher oil prices could take the rate to 6.50–6.75%. Inflation was 4.3% in September, within the 3–5% target; energy, fuel and utilities inflation was 7.7%. Reserves are above USD 6bn, or 4.3 months of imports. The shilling averaged TZS 2,655 per dollar in the third quarter, 1.3% weaker than the previous quarter.
Already signalled Energy-driven inflation had been rising since mid-year (from 4.0% in June to 4.3%), which prompted the July rise. The gold purchase programme began in late 2024 as a reserves measure.
Where the reads disagree The central bank is tightening while growth is projected to accelerate, so financing costs rise just as expansion is pushed. Gold rules serve reserves first and miners second.
Forward-look The baseline is a hold at 6.25% into January. A further rise is the risk case if oil or El Niño pushes inflation above 5%.
What would change this read Level falls if inflation stays below 4.5% and the shilling holds. It rises if the January decision raises the rate.

The Bank of Tanzania keeps its rate at 6.25% for the first quarter of 2027.

Uganda 4.0 High · Deteriorating
Coverage partial, 2 of 3 layers
Signal The Bank of Uganda raised its rate from 9.75% to 12% at an emergency meeting on 14 October and lifted the cash reserve requirement to 13.5%. The shilling kept falling afterwards, touching about 4,500 per dollar on 23 October and losing more than 10% since late August. Inflation rose to 5.8% in October from 4.6%, the fastest since June 2023; core inflation rose to 4.4%. More than USD 200m of portfolio money left local bonds.
Already signalled The shilling first broke 4,000 on 5 October, nine days before the emergency meeting. Food-crop prices were up 10.7% before the October inflation print.
Where the reads disagree Rate rises target inflation driven mainly by imported fuel and food, and the currency kept falling after the hike, so the tool may not reach the cause.
Forward-look The rate rise alone has not held the currency, so further dollar sales or tightening are likely while outflows continue.
What would change this read Direction moves to Stable if outflows stop and the shilling holds below 4,200 for several weeks.

Rwanda 3.3 Elevated · Deteriorating
Coverage partial, 2 of 3 layers
Signal Urban inflation rose to 17.2% in September from 15.7%, against a 2–8% target. Core inflation was 11.4% and energy inflation 45.0%. The National Bank of Rwanda raised its rate to 8.75% on 26 August, a cumulative 175 bp since November 2025. Its annual report on 6 November shows growth of 10.6% in 2025/26 and average inflation of 9.2% over that year.
Already signalled Inflation had been climbing all year: 9.1% in the first quarter, 13.2% in the second, 14.5% in July. Each rate rise followed the data rather than leading it.
Where the reads disagree Growth near 10% and strong exports sit beside inflation that is still accelerating. Activity and prices pull in opposite directions.
Forward-look The central bank expects inflation to return to target only in the second half of 2027. With prices still accelerating, another rate rise at the next meeting is likely.
What would change this read Direction moves to Stable when monthly inflation stops rising. It moves to Improving when inflation is back inside 2–8%.

The National Bank of Rwanda raises its rate above 8.75% at its next policy meeting.
Resolves by 31 December 2026
Zambia 2.6 Moderate · Improving
Coverage full, 3 of 3 layers
Signal IMF staff agreed a USD 1.47bn, 36-month programme on 9 October, subject to Board approval and prior actions. It requires reversing temporary fuel-tax relief, more transparent mining licensing and a primary surplus rising to 3% of GDP by 2029. The 2027 budget targets growth of about 5% and a deficit of no more than 3% of GDP, down from 5.9%. The Bank of Zambia cut its rate by 250 bp to 10.75% on 30 September. Inflation was 6.3% in October, up from 6.1%.
Already signalled Inflation fell from 11.2% in December 2025 to 6.1% by September, and the Bank of Zambia cut four times in a row, so the improvement was under way before the IMF deal.
Where the reads disagree Output targets and rate cuts point up, while fiscal tightening, the end of fuel relief and local-content rules raise costs. October’s inflation uptick is the first sign of the trade-off.
Forward-look Board approval and the fuel-tax reversal are the next markers. Removing fuel relief will push inflation up in early 2027.
What would change this read Direction moves to Stable if Board approval slips past year-end or inflation leaves the 6–8% band after fuel relief is removed.
75% Dated call
The IMF Executive Board approves Zambia’s 36-month programme by 31 December 2026.
